## T1 {slot: hook, fig: fig-01}
Every cable that runs into a sealed machine drags grit in with it, and the usual cure is a shutdown and a pair of tweezers.[narrative]
This filing claims a way to make the grit stay outside.[M7]

## T2 {slot: stop, fig: fig-02}
The housing does the ordinary thing here: it defines a guide channel that carries the cable through the wall of the enclosure.[M1]
What the claim adds sits at the mouth of that channel, a wiper collar that the cable has to pass through on its way in.[M2]

## T3 {slot: stop, fig: fig-03}
The collar is not a seal in the usual sense, because it never grips the cable and never has to be squeezed tight against it.[M2]
It stands proud of the mouth instead, so the grit riding in on the cable is wiped off outside the wall rather than inside the box.[M7]

## T4 {slot: pivot, fig: fig-03}
Here is the admission tucked into the dependent claim: the collar is an elastomeric sleeve, which is to say a part that is meant to wear.[M3]
The drawing puts that sleeve on the outer face, where a hand can reach it without opening the enclosure at all.[M9]

## T5 {slot: landing, fig: fig-01}
So the invention is smaller than the problem it answers, and more candid about itself than most.[narrative]
It buys back the maintenance stop by admitting up front that one soft part is there to be replaced.[M8]
